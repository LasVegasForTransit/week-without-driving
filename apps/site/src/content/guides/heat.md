---
title: 'Walk or roll in the heat'
order: 3
icon: 'mdi:weather-sunny'
cardSummary: 'Stay cool and safe on the way to the bus, whether you walk or roll.'
intro: >-
  Early October afternoons in Las Vegas can still pass 90°F. Whether you walk, use a wheelchair or
  walker, or push a stroller, a little planning makes the trip to the bus stop cooler and safer.
steps:
  - heading: 'Pick a cooler time and bring water'
    icon: 'mdi:water-outline'
    body:
      - >-
        When you can, make your trip in the morning or after 5 pm. Bring water for everyone in your
        group, and wear a hat and light-colored clothes.
      - >-
        Some medicines make it harder for your body to handle heat. If you take any medicine, ask
        your pharmacist.
      - >-
        Feeling dizzy or sick? Stop somewhere cool and drink water before you go any further.
  - heading: 'Stay on the shady side and plan places to rest'
    icon: 'shade-diagram'
    body:
      - >-
        Buildings, walls and trees cast shade away from the sun. On a north-south street, that puts
        shade on the east sidewalk in the morning and on the west sidewalk in the afternoon. On an
        east-west street, the south side usually has more shade all day.
      - >-
        Checking a route ahead of time? A map app's satellite view shows dark green patches of trees
        along the sidewalk.
      - >-
        Plan places to cool down along the way: bus shelters, libraries, community centers, and
        covered walkways at shopping centers. If part of your trip has no shade for a long stretch,
        a bus may be able to carry you past it.
  - heading: 'Watch for hot surfaces'
    icon: 'mdi:weather-sunny-alert'
    body:
      - >-
        Metal and dark surfaces left in the sun get hot enough to burn: wheelchair handrims and
        seats, stroller handles, seats and buckles, and metal benches at bus stops.
      - >-
        Gloves help with hot handrims. A light-colored cloth over a seat left in the sun keeps it
        cooler. Touch a stroller seat or buckle with the back of your hand before a child sits down.
      - >-
        Hot pavement can burn a service animal's or pet's paws, too.
