---
title: 'Walk or roll in the heat'
order: 4
icon: 'mdi:weather-sunny'
cardSummary: 'Stay cool and safe on the way to the bus, whether you walk or roll.'
intro: >-
  October afternoons in Las Vegas can still pass 90°F. A little planning makes the trip to the bus
  stop cooler and safer, whether you walk, roll, or push a stroller.
steps:
  - heading: 'Pick a cooler time and bring water'
    icon: 'mdi:water-outline'
    body:
      - >-
        Go in the morning or after 5 pm if you can. Bring water for everyone. Wear a hat and light
        colors.
      - >-
        Some medicines make heat harder on your body. If you take medicine, ask your pharmacist.
      - >-
        Feeling dizzy or sick? Stop somewhere cool and drink water before you go on.
  - heading: 'Stay on the shady side and plan places to rest'
    icon: 'shade-diagram'
    body:
      - >-
        Buildings and trees make shade on the side away from the sun. On a north-south street, the
        east sidewalk has shade in the morning. The west sidewalk has shade in the afternoon. On an
        east-west street, the south side usually has more shade.
      - >-
        Planning ahead? A map app's satellite view shows trees as dark green spots along the
        sidewalk.
      - >-
        Plan places to cool down, like bus shelters, libraries, community centers and covered
        walkways. A long stretch with no shade? A bus may get you past it.
  - heading: 'Watch for hot surfaces'
    icon: 'mdi:weather-sunny-alert'
    body:
      - >-
        Metal and dark things in the sun can get hot enough to burn. Watch out for wheelchair
        handrims and seats, stroller handles, seats and buckles, and metal benches at bus stops.
      - >-
        Gloves help with hot handrims. A light cloth over a seat keeps it cooler. Touch a stroller
        seat or buckle with the back of your hand before a child sits down.
      - >-
        Hot pavement can burn an animal's paws, too.
---
